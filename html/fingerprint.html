What fingerprinting is

Every browser leaks small facts: the exact pixels it draws for a line of text, your graphics card, installed fonts, screen size, time zone. None is unique on its own. Combined, they usually are. Studies by AmIUnique and the EFF (the team behind Cover Your Tracks) found that most browsers they tested were unique.

That matters for Ad Poison because a fingerprint survives everything on the reset and hygiene pages. Clear cookies, reset your ad ID, and a fingerprinting script can still say “same browser as last week” and reattach your old profile. Blurring your fingerprint keeps those resets from being undone.

Works with the generator: chaff fills the profile tied to your cookies with junk. Fingerprint protection stops trackers linking a fresh, clean session back to the old one. They cover different holes.

Two ways to blur

Pick one approach. Mixing them, or stacking extensions, tends to make you rarer.

=

Blend in

Make every user look identical, so your fingerprint is shared with a huge crowd. This is the strongest approach, but it only works if you don't customize: no extra extensions, default window size, default settings.

Who does it: Tor Browser, Mullvad Browser, and Firefox's resistFingerprinting mode.

?

Randomize

Add small, invisible changes to the values scripts read, so you look like a different person each session. It's easier to live with because you keep your normal browser habits, but the noise itself can sometimes be detected.

Who does it: Brave (built in), JShelter, CanvasBlocker, Chromixer.

Our picks, easiest first

A browser with protection built in beats any extension: it's tested by millions and updates itself.

About Chromixer

Chromixer is an open-source (MIT) Chrome extension that randomizes canvas, WebGL, audio, fonts, screen and hardware values, WebRTC, client rects and more, with a new random seed each session. On paper it covers more ground than most extensions, and you can run this page's check to watch it work.

Why it's last on the list: At the time of writing (October 2026) it isn't in the Chrome Web Store. You install it by downloading the source and loading it unpacked in Developer mode, so it never updates itself and Chrome warns about it on every launch. It can read and change every page you visit, and it's a young project that few people have reviewed. Randomizing values one by one can also produce combinations no real device has (a phone screen with a desktop GPU), which can make you easier to spot.

If you're comfortable reading the code and pulling updates yourself, it's a fun one to try and a good way to see how randomization works. Everyone else should start with Brave or JShelter.

Turn it on

Brave
  • Settings → Shields: make sure Block fingerprinting is on (it is by default).
  • Leave your language list at one entry. Extra languages add identifying detail.
  • Test it: scan here, restart Brave, scan again. Canvas, WebGL and audio should all show Changed.
Firefox
  • Settings → Privacy & Security → Enhanced Tracking Protection → Strict. This blocks known and suspected fingerprinters.
  • Optional, stronger: in about:config set privacy.resistFingerprinting to true. Your time zone reads as UTC, canvas reads are blocked or randomized, and some sites break. Mozilla doesn't recommend it for everyday use, so try it in a separate profile first.
Safari
  • Mac: Safari → Settings → Advanced → Use advanced tracking and fingerprinting protection → in all browsing.
  • iPhone and iPad: Settings → Apps → Safari → Advanced → Advanced Tracking and Fingerprinting Protection → All Browsing.
Tor Browser & Mullvad Browser
  • Don't install extensions or change fonts. Every tweak moves you out of the crowd.
  • Don't maximize the window. Letterboxing keeps your window size in common buckets; a maximized window reveals your screen.
  • Raise the security level to Safer or Safest to disable more scripting when you don't need it.
Chrome & Edge
  • No built-in fingerprint protection for normal browsing. Install JShelter from your browser's store, or move your everyday browsing to Brave.
  • Edge's Strict tracking prevention (see hygiene) blocks some known fingerprinting scripts, but doesn't change what your browser reports.

Quick answers

Does a VPN stop browser fingerprinting?

No. A VPN hides your IP address, but your fingerprint is built from your browser, graphics card, fonts and settings, which look the same through any network.

Should I install several anti-fingerprinting extensions?

No. Every extension changes how your browser behaves, and stacking them makes you rarer, not safer. Pick one approach: a privacy browser, or one extension.

Is randomizing or blending in better?

Blending in (Tor Browser, Mullvad Browser) is strongest because millions of users share one fingerprint. Randomizing (Brave) is easier to live with day to day and still stops trackers linking your sessions.
