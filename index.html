    Step 01 · More noise

    Noise beyond this tab

    The generator stops when you close the tab, and it can't reach your phone. These independent open-source projects can. Add one at a time, check what access it asks for, and keep in mind that automated browsing can break a site's terms of service.

    AdNauseam

    Browser extension

    Blocks ads, then quietly clicks them in the background, so your profile fills up with ads you never cared about.

    This is the one thing Ad Poison won't do. Advertisers can end up paying for those clicks. The developers say it isn't click fraud, but the decision is yours, and you can reduce or turn off clicking in its settings.

    Use it in place of your other ad blocker, which would hide ads before AdNauseam sees them. Google doesn't allow it in the Chrome Web Store, so Chromium browsers need a manual install.

    Firefox add-on adnauseam.io

    Fauxx

    Android app

    Generates believable decoy browsing from your phone, which a tab on your computer can't do. Get it from F-Droid or GitHub; it isn't on Google Play.

    If you use a tracker blocker such as DuckDuckGo App Tracking Protection, exclude Fauxx from it, or much of the noise gets blocked.

    Its own docs say the location spoofing module doesn't do what the name suggests, and that it can't change your device fingerprint or undo matches on your email or phone number.

    Fauxx on GitHub

    Fauxx Desktop

    Windows, macOS, Linux

    The computer companion to Fauxx. It runs decoy personas in an isolated Chromium profile, can pair with the phone app over your home network so the decoys match, and includes tools to check whether the noise is working.

    It's still early, so expect changes. Downloads are unsigned, so Windows and macOS warn on first launch. Verify the published checksum first.

    Fauxx Desktop on GitHub

    TrackMeNot

    Browser extension

    The academic project that popularized decoy searches. It sends randomized queries to search engines in the background, but it reportedly hasn't been updated since 2019.

    TrackMeNot-Fork keeps the idea going on Firefox. It's small, and its author says it was rebuilt with AI help, so read the source before giving it access to every site.

    trackmenot.io

    ISP Data Pollution

    Python script

    Crawls random sites from your machine to bury your history at the network level. It's aimed at what your internet provider sees rather than ad cookies.

    For tinkerers: it needs Python, Selenium and ChromeDriver, it's built to use a lot of bandwidth, and some install notes are dated. The author warns that this kind of noise can be filtered statistically and can't hide specific sensitive visits.

    ISP Data Pollution on GitHub

    FAQ

    Questions from the crypt

    Does generating fake browsing traffic actually work?

    Partly. Ad systems are good at spotting pure randomness, so Ad Poison defaults to persona mode: coherent, plausible interests that look like a real person. Researchers have long shown that obfuscation tools such as TrackMeNot and AdNauseam degrade profile quality. Noise works best when combined with blocking trackers and switching personalization off, which is why this site covers all three.

    Is Ad Poison safe and private?

    It's a static website: no analytics, no cookies, no server, and even the fonts are self-hosted. Your generator settings and checklists are saved only in your browser's local storage. Read the privacy notice.

    Will Google show me a CAPTCHA?

    It can if searches come too fast. Every interval gets ±40% random jitter. Keep the pace at 20 seconds or slower and mix in several destinations. If a CAPTCHA appears in the chaff window, just stop, wait a bit, and try a slower pace.

    Why not a browser extension like Chaff?

    The original Chaff extension no longer works in Chrome, and traffic-generating extensions keep getting pulled from stores. A plain web page works in any browser, needs no install, and you can read every line of its source. If you want noise that keeps running after you close this tab or that comes from your phone, see more noise tools.

    Is it legal to poison my ad profile?

    Visiting public pages and opting out of personalization from your own device is generally lawful. Ad Poison never clicks ads (no click fraud) and never touches anyone else's accounts. If you're unsure, check your local laws and each site's terms of service.

    How unique is my browser anyway?

    Test your fingerprint at AmIUnique or the EFF's Cover Your Tracks. Or run our private fingerprint check, which runs entirely in your browser and shows whether your fingerprint changes between sessions. Fingerprinting works even without cookies, so pair it with the browser hygiene guide.
