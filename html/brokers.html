Delete yourself from data brokers

Data brokers buy, scrape and sell your name, addresses, phone numbers, relatives and shopping habits. Advertisers (Meta included) match against those lists. Services like Incogni, DeleteMe and Cloaked charge a subscription to send opt-outs and hand out masked emails. Everything they do, you can do for free. It just takes an afternoon.

Updated October 22, 2026

What the paid services do, and the free way

Paid services are worth it if your time is worth more than the fee, especially for repeat removals. They don't have special powers: they use the same opt-out forms and privacy laws you can.

1. Mask first, so it doesn't refill

Removal is pointless if every new account hands out your real email and phone again. A different alias per site also tells you exactly who leaked or sold you. This is the Cloaked part, free.

  • DuckDuckGo Email Protection (unlimited, free) · Firefox Relay (free tier) · SimpleLogin (free tier) · addy.io (free tier, open source)

  • Google Voice (US, free)

3. Opt out of the big people-search sites

Search each site for yourself first and copy your listing's URL; most forms ask for it. Use an alias email for the confirmation link. Never upload ID unless the law in your area requires it for that request.

  • Spokeo opt-out

  • PeopleFinders opt-out

  • USPhoneBook · Nuwber · That's Them · PeekYou
