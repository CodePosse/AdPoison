    FAQ

    Questions from the crypt

    Does generating fake browsing traffic actually work?

    Partly. Ad systems are good at spotting pure randomness, so Ad Poison defaults to persona mode: coherent, plausible interests that look like a real person. Researchers have long shown that obfuscation tools such as TrackMeNot and AdNauseam degrade profile quality. Noise works best when combined with blocking trackers and switching personalization off, which is why this site covers all three.

    Is Ad Poison safe and private?

    It's a static website: no analytics, no cookies, no server, and even the fonts are self-hosted. Your generator settings and checklists are saved only in your browser's local storage. Read the privacy notice.

    Will Google show me a CAPTCHA?

    It can if searches come too fast. Every interval gets ±40% random jitter. Keep the pace at 20 seconds or slower and mix in several destinations. If a CAPTCHA appears in the chaff window, just stop, wait a bit, and try a slower pace.

    Why not a browser extension like Chaff?

    The original Chaff extension no longer works in Chrome, and traffic-generating extensions keep getting pulled from stores. A plain web page works in any browser, needs no install, and you can read every line of its source.

    Is it legal to poison my ad profile?

    Visiting public pages and opting out of personalization from your own device is generally lawful. Ad Poison never clicks ads (no click fraud) and never touches anyone else's accounts. If you're unsure, check your local laws and each site's terms of service.

    How unique is my browser anyway?

    Test your fingerprint at AmIUnique or the EFF's Cover Your Tracks. Or run our private fingerprint check, which runs entirely in your browser and shows whether your fingerprint changes between sessions. Fingerprinting works even without cookies, so pair it with the browser hygiene guide.
